Amateur Hour

Working name. A San Francisco membership for trying one entirely new thing every week. Members get two options, pick one, and show up to a small hands-on class.

The idea

Who it's for

People in the city whose work and evenings are mostly screens, and who feel they've stopped learning things from scratch.

What they buy

A weekly habit, like a gym membership. The class changes every week and they don't have to find it, vet it or organise anyone.

What we refuse to build

A catalogue, reviews, ratings, filters. Two options a week keeps the decision to about ten seconds.

Why liking it isn't the goal

Members will dislike some classes. The pitch is the practice of being a beginner, so a bad week still counts.

Nearest neighbour

Reasonal sells "the antidote to brain rot" as lessons inside an iOS app, with a "made by humans, for humans" stance. Same enemy, but their fix is more phone time.

Where we differ

Ours happens in a room with other people and your hands busy. The phone's only job is one text on Sunday, and that's worth saying out loud on the landing page.

How a week works

Sunday evening

Two options appear in your account (not by text). Usually one that's physical or outdoors and one that's made by hand or on a stage.

By Tuesday

Tap to pick. Add a friend on a day pass. If contacts are synced, you see who you know is going.

That week

Show up. Six to twelve people, everything provided, about two hours.

After

One question, "again or never again?" It tunes next week's pair and nobody else sees it.

Sign-up, in under a minute

Account

Phone number, then a texted code, then first name. No password and no email.

What you already do

Tap the things you've done more than twice (climbing, pottery, cooking classes, an instrument). We use it to steer away from them.

Four quick either-ors

Happy to sweat or rather not. Outdoors in the cold or indoors. Good with your hands or all thumbs. Fine on a stage or would rather die.

Hard nos and logistics

Heights, open water, raw seafood, alcohol. Then which evenings work and whether weekend mornings do.

Round 1: four directions

Kept for reference. Version 2 takes the letter from 3, the colours and card from 1 and the choice from 2. Direction 4 is dropped.

1. Punch card

Closest to Granola's off-white and green. The gym idea made literal: a rec-centre membership card that fills up, with the member's own verdicts written on it.

2. This or that

The liveliest, nearest ClassPass. The hero is this week's real choice and you can pick one right there.

3. The letter

The quietest, nearest Hinge and Instinct. A short letter in a book serif with one pen underline and a handwritten list.

4. Pole flyer

The most counter-culture. A photocopied telephone-pole flyer on goldenrod paper, and you tear off a tab to join.

Things to decide

  • The name. Others worth a look: New Tricks, Firsts, Beginner's Club.
  • Price. Now $50 a month for 12 credits, with classes costing 2 to 5 credits, unused credits rolling over and a free pause. That copies ClassPass, which sells credit plans from 8 credits for $19 to 125 for $249 a month, prices each class in credits by demand, rolls unused credits over up to your plan size and lets you pause. The catch for us: these classes retail at $75 to $200, so $50 for roughly four of them only works if providers sell us unsold seats well below retail. I couldn't find what ClassPass pays studios.
  • Calendar. The confirmation screen asks members to connect a calendar. If most do, it could replace the "when are you free" question.
  • Who runs the room. Buying seats in existing classes is fast to start; private Amateur Hour sessions make the group feel like a club.
  • How the pair is built. One body and one hands is my suggestion, so the two never feel like the same choice.
  • Reward. A full punch card (12 classes) earns a free week. Decide whether that is a free class on top or a week of membership credited.
  • Skipping. Can a member pause a week without losing it?
